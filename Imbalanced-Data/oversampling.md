What is oversampling?
Instead of throwing away majority class data, oversampling does the opposite: duplicate minority class data until both classes are roughly equal in size.
Back to the fraud example: 99,500 normal, 500 fraud. Instead of shrinking the normal transactions down to 500, oversampling would take those 500 fraud examples and copy them repeatedly until you have roughly 99,500 "fraud" rows too (each fraud case appearing many times over). Now the dataset is balanced at 99,500 / 99,500 — but you got there by adding data, not removing it.
Why would this help?
Same underlying goal as undersampling — force the model to stop ignoring the minority class. If fraud cases now make up half the dataset (even if it's the same 500 examples repeated many times), the model can no longer get away with mostly ignoring them, since fraud mistakes now matter just as much to its overall score as normal-transaction mistakes.
Simple analogy
Back to the cat/dog example: 995 cat photos, 5 dog photos. Oversampling is like taking those same 5 dog photos and showing them to the learner 199 times each, so by the end they've seen dog examples just as often as cat examples in total — even though there are really still only 5 unique dogs.
The catch — and it's a different catch than undersampling's
You're not losing data this time — you keep everything. But here's the new problem: you're not adding any new information either. You're just showing the model the exact same 500 fraud examples over and over and over again.
This creates a real risk called overfitting (you've actually seen this term before, with max_depth in Random Forests — same concept, different cause here). The model can start to "memorize" those exact 500 fraud cases very precisely — down to their exact, specific quirks — rather than learning the general patterns that make something fraudulent. Then when a brand new, never-before-seen type of fraud shows up (one that doesn't closely resemble those 500 specific examples), the model may fail to catch it, because it didn't really learn "what fraud generally looks like" — it learned "what these 500 specific transactions look like."
Simple analogy for the overfitting risk
Imagine a student who has only 5 practice questions for an exam topic, so they just memorize the answers to those exact 5 questions by repeating them to themselves 100 times. On exam day, if 3 of those exact questions show up, they'll get them right. But if the exam has different-but-related questions on the same topic, they'll likely fail, because they never learned the underlying concept — just those specific memorized answers.
